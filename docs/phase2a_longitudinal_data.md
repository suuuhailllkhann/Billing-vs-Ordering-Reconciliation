# Phase 2A synthetic longitudinal data contract

All records generated by this phase are fictional. The simulated distributions are
engineering assumptions for testing and research; they are not estimates of any real
pharmacy, patient population, medication, insurer, adherence pattern, or refill rate.

## Tables

- `patients.csv`: synthetic identity/display data keyed by `patient_id`.
- `medications.csv`: fictional medication IDs, NDC-like strings, and invented names.
- `prescriptions.csv`: patient-medication prescriptions/Rx numbers, exact NDC,
  prescribed quantity, days supply, and authorized refill count.
- `fills.csv`: longitudinal billing/fill events, including refill accounting and
  fictional insurance/BIN fields.
- `refill_observations.csv`: point-in-time historical features and the future label.
- `integrity_summary.json`: generated counts and integrity checks.

The default fixed seed is `20260814`. The study window is 2025-01-01 through
2026-06-30 (approximately 18 months). IDs beginning with `SYN-`, names containing an
explicit fictional marker, fictional medication names, and NDC-like values beginning
with `9` are deliberate safeguards against confusion with operational records.

## Simulation assumptions

- 1,500 patients and 12 medications are generated by default. The larger population
  supports ML experimentation without changing the simulation's event probabilities.
- Each patient receives one to four distinct medication episodes.
- Each patient receives one fixed hidden behavior profile: 40% Consistent, 40%
  Average, and 20% Inconsistent. The profile is retained only in the synthetic patient
  master table for generator audit and is never exported to ML observations.
- Initial prescriptions begin during the first year so repeated histories can form.
- Days supply is selected from 7, 14, 30, 60, or 90 days, with 30 days most common.
- Most patients receive one unit per day; a minority receive two simulated units.
- Each prescription authorizes one to five refills, in addition to fill number zero.
- Refill timing is sampled as early/on-time/late using profile-specific weights:
  Consistent 15%/75%/10%, Average 20%/55%/25%, and Inconsistent 15%/30%/55%.
  Timing offsets are bounded and fill dates remain chronological.
- At each refill opportunity, some histories stop. This represents missed fills or no
  observed subsequent fill without asserting why the event was absent.
- After authorized refills are exhausted, some episodes receive a new fictional
  prescription and continue. Days supply may change at renewal.
- Renewal starts from profile-specific probabilities of 75%, 60%, or 40%, then uses
  only known refill history: historical on-time rate (+10/0/-10 points), latest timing
  (0/+5/-5 points for early/on-time/late), and prior-fill count (0/+2/+5 points for
  1–2/3–5/6+ fills). The result is clamped to 0–1 and sampled with the seeded RNG.
- Billed quantity usually equals the simulated prescribed quantity but may vary by one
  daily unit to create controlled quantity variation.
- Each patient has a fictional primary plan; a minority of fills use another fictional
  plan to represent coverage changes.
- A patient-medication episode is capped at 14 fills to keep the dataset compact.

No clinical dosing, therapeutic equivalence, diagnosis, prescriber, inventory, or
real-world adherence behavior is represented.

## Prediction observation and target

Only fills with `current_refills_remaining == 0` are candidates. For each candidate,
`expected_supply_end_date = fill_date + days_supply` and `observation_date =
expected_supply_end_date - 10 days`.

`prescription_renewal_within_window` equals 1 when a different, newly issued
`prescription_id` for the same `patient_id + NDC` has a prescription date strictly
after observation and no later than `expected_supply_end_date + 7 days`. The lower
boundary is exclusive because a new Rx known on the observation date removes the need
to predict. Ordinary fills under the same Rx and prescriptions for other NDCs do not
qualify.

Rows whose +7-day target window extends after study end are censored, never negative.
A candidate is excluded when its observation precedes its fill or when a qualifying
new Rx was already received on or before observation. Future Rx identity and receipt
date are used only to build the target and are never exported.

## Point-in-time features

Current-fill facts retained for eligible observations:

- `current_quantity_billed`
- `current_days_supply`
- `current_refill_number`
- `prescription_age_days`

Patient-medication history available by that date:

- `previous_fill_count`
- `days_since_previous_fill`
- `average_previous_refill_interval_days`
- `std_previous_refill_interval_days`
- `latest_refill_timing_gap_days`
- `average_previous_timing_gap_days`
- `previous_early_fill_rate`
- `previous_on_time_fill_rate`

Medication-wide history uses only fills on earlier calendar dates:

- `medication_prior_fill_count`
- `medication_prior_average_days_supply`
- `medication_prior_average_quantity`

Observation, patient, prescription, medication, and NDC identifiers are retained only
for auditing, grouping, and future temporal splitting. `current_refills_remaining` is
retained as an audit/eligibility field. None is a modeling feature.

## Insufficient history

The first patient-medication fill has `previous_fill_count = 0`. Interval, refill
timing, and prior-rate features are missing because no legitimate historical value
exists. Standard deviation remains missing until at least two historical intervals
exist. Medication-wide averages are missing when no earlier-date medication fills are
available. Missingness is preserved rather than filled with fabricated history.

## Leakage exclusions

The feature table excludes patient name, DOB, future fill date, days until the next
fill, future prescription data, future refill state, and target-derived fields.
Patient-medication features are computed incrementally from that history only.
Medication-wide aggregates use strictly earlier dates, so same-day row ordering cannot
leak information. The observation date is retained to support future chronological
train/validation/test splitting; no random split is created in Phase 2A.

## Business-rule separation

The future application workflow is: flagged patient → Prescription Review → staff may
mark `New Prescription Received` or `Follow Up With Doctor`. These are future workflow
states only. They are not generated labels or ML features, and this phase implements
no prescriber contact or automated outreach.
